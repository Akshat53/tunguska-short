# Tunguska — 60s cartoon short (cartoon-3d-short pipeline)

## Facts + sources (verified before writing)
- 30 June 1908, 00:14:28 UT (≈07:14 local), near Podkamennaya Tunguska River, central Siberia (60°53′N 101°54′E). — Gifco et al., "A Multidisciplinary Investigation in the Site of the Tunguska Explosion" (Tunguska99, Univ. of Bologna, 1999)
- Object disrupted 5–10 km above ground (airburst); energy 10–20 megatons TNT. No crater, no macroscopic fragments ever found. — Tunguska99; NASA "115 Years Ago: The Tunguska Asteroid Impact Event"
- Ground zero: trees left standing but stripped of branches and bark ("telegraph pole" trees); from ~3–10 km out, trees blown over with tops pointing away. — NASA; PSI.edu (Hartmann), "1908 Siberia Explosion"
- "80 million trees" is a ~4× overestimate (Astapovich's preliminary guess mis-cited for a century); use "millions of trees". — GSA 2024 abstract "Myths and Misinformation about Tunguska and Cosmic Airbursts"; Bunch et al. rebuttal (Sci Rep, 2025)
- Butterfly-shaped destruction area ≈ 830 sq mi (~2,150 km²). — NASA
- Evenki herders ~30 km away: tents blown into the air, reindeer killed; heat flash singed bark and set small fires within ~30 km; one man thrown ~40 ft into a tree, later died. — PSI.edu
- Barometric shock wave detected by barographs around the world (reported to have circled the globe twice). — common record in the literature; kept as spoken line "the shockwave circled the planet. Twice."
- 2016: UN proclaimed 30 June International Asteroid Day; NASA Planetary Defense Coordination Office + DART mission. — NASA
- NOT USED (debunked myth): "if it had arrived 4 h 47 min later it would have destroyed St. Petersburg" — false (ignores Earth's orbital motion). — GSA 2024 abstract

## Narration (~3.7 words/s; target 55–70 s; steady, calm voice)

HOOK:
On the thirtieth of June, nineteen-oh-eight, the sky exploded over Siberia — and nothing ever hit the ground.

SETTING:
Just taiga. A forest of thin pines, a slow river, Evenki herders with their reindeer. A morning like any other.

ACTION:
Then a light brighter than the sun, and a sound like gunfire — but there was no gun. A rock the size of a building had hit the atmosphere at fifteen kilometers a second.

ESCALATION:
At that speed, air cannot get out of the way. It squeezes and squeezes, and the rock gives up — it exploded five to ten kilometers above the forest.

SNAP:
The blast wave slammed down and flattened everything. Millions of trees, knocked over like grass. Tents thrown into the air. The shockwave circled the planet. Twice.

DEADPAN (double line):
And directly underneath? Trees still standing. Stripped bare — but standing.

EXPLANATION + ANALOGY:
Because the force came from straight above. A tree is strongest the way a straw is strongest — end on. Squeeze it from the top, it holds. Push it sideways, it folds. The blast blew the outer forest sideways. The middle, it only pressed.

CONSEQUENCE:
No crater. No rock. Scientists took almost twenty years to reach the site. Now the thirtieth of June is International Asteroid Day, and there is a whole office at NASA whose job is watching the sky.

CALLBACK (twist):
That morning, a city-sized blast hit the emptiest place on Earth. It will not always be this lucky.

## Tone rules
- Straight narration, one steady voice. No drama voice-acting.
- Jokes are real facts (standing trees under the blast; "twice").
- No in-scene text/labels.

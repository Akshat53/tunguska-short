**Alternative titles:** The Tanganyika Laughter Epidemic (1962) | 1,000 People Couldn't Stop Laughing | The Epidemic Whose Only Symptom Was Laughter | Why 95 Schoolgirls Couldn't Stop Laughing

**Description:**

On 30 January 1962, at a mission girls' boarding school in Kashasha, near Bukoba on Lake Victoria in newly independent Tanganyika (now Tanzania), three students started laughing and couldn't stop. Within weeks 95 of the school's 159 pupils had attacks: laughing mixed with crying, restlessness and fainting, lasting hours and coming back. Teachers were not affected.

On 18 March the school was closed and the girls were sent home. It spread with them: to Nshamba, where 217 people had attacks, and to other villages and schools. In all, 14 schools were shut and about 1,000 people were affected over roughly a year and a half.

Doctors tested for poison and infection and found neither. The leading explanation is mass psychogenic illness: real physical symptoms, spread socially, triggered by stress. Tanganyika had won independence on 9 December 1961, and pupils lived under strict discipline and heavy expectations. No joke was ever recorded. This is the leading theory, not a settled fact, and some historians question how the case was framed at the time.

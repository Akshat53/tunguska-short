## C Research report
Full notes: `research.md`; claims with quotes and URLs: `research.json` (22 claims, 2 marked debunked/NOT USED).
- **Facts:** 15 January 1919, Commercial Street, North End, Boston. Tank ~50 ft tall, 90 ft across, up to ~2.3 M US gal (C03). Wave ~35 mph (C04). 21 dead, ~150 injured (C02).
  Molasses ~1.5x denser than water (C06); thickens sharply in cold, viscosity up 4x+ (Sharp 2016, C07); about half the victims died because stuck (Rubinstein, C08).
  Leaks from 1915; kids collected drips (C10); painted brown to hide leaks (C11). USIA found liable by auditor Hugh Ogden (C12). Boston required filed calculations and
  signed stamped drawings; states licensed engineers (C13).
- **Context:** USIA bought Purity Distilling in 1917; molasses was fermented into industrial alcohol; the day was mild (~40 F) after a cold spell, freezing that night (C09).
- **Disputed:** wave height (8-40 ft), settlement ($628k vs $1M), time of day, who ordered the paint (C05, C12, C18, C11 notes).
- **NOT USED (debunked/unsupported):** "the North End still smells of molasses" as fact (C21; folklore C20 in pinned comment only); any exact wave height as fact (C22); waist-deep rescuers, wagons, named ships (not verified this session).

**Description**

On 15 January 1919, a storage tank holding about 2.3 million gallons of molasses burst in Boston's North End. The wave, estimated at around 35 mph and somewhere between 15 and 25 feet high (estimates range from 8 to 40 ft), killed 21 people and injured about 150. Molasses is far denser than water, and as it cooled in the January air it thickened, trapping victims and slowing rescuers. Neighbours said the tank had leaked since it was filled in 1915; instead of fixing it, the company had it painted brown. A court-appointed auditor later found the owner, United States Industrial Alcohol, liable, and Boston began requiring engineers' calculations to be filed and plans signed and stamped.

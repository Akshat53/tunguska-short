## Subject summary
Around midday on 15 January 1919, a 50-ft-tall, 90-ft-wide steel tank on Commercial Street in Boston's North End, holding about 2.3 million US gallons
of molasses (for industrial alcohol), split open. A wave of molasses, estimated at around 35 mph and somewhere between 15 and 25 ft high (estimates
range 8-40 ft), crushed buildings and swept through the neighbourhood: 21 people died and about 150 were injured. Molasses is ~1.5x denser than water,
and as it cooled in the January air it thickened sharply, trapping victims and hampering rescuers (Harvard fluid-dynamics study, 2016). The tank had
leaked since it was filled in 1915; residents complained and children collected the drips; the company had the tank painted brown to disguise the leaks.
After years of hearings, a court-appointed auditor (Hugh W. Ogden) found the owner, United States Industrial Alcohol (which had bought Purity Distilling
in 1917), liable — rejecting its anarchist-bomb defence. Boston then required engineers' calculations to be filed with plans and stamped drawings to be
signed; Massachusetts and most states passed laws certifying engineers.

**Central question:** how does a tank of syrup kill twenty-one people? **Answer:** weight (dense, fast), cold (it thickened and trapped people), and
warnings ignored (leaks painted over) — which is why engineers now sign for their math.

## Disputed / conflicts
- Wave height: 8-15 ft (Straight Dope), 15 ft (Mass Moments, HISTORY.com, Park), 25 ft (SciAm via Wikipedia, NBC/Puleo), 30 ft (Boston Post 1919), 40 ft (plaque).
- Speed: 35 mph widely agreed (an estimate; MIT theoretical max 38 mph); one 2016 report cited 16 km/h (snippet only).
- Settlement: $628,000 (Wikipedia) / "more than $600,000" (Straight Dope) vs $1,000,000 ordered (Mass Moments).
- Time: ~12:30 (Wikipedia) / ~1 pm (Boston.gov) / "midafternoon" (Mass Moments). Not spoken.
- Who painted it: Boston.gov says Purity Distilling; Puleo/NBC says the USIA manager. Narration says "the company".
- "About half died because stuck": one expert quote (Rubinstein, CSM) -> softened to "many victims".

# Research notes — Battle of Saraighat (1671)

**Research mode: web-verified** (WebSearch + WebFetch, 2026-11-01). Limits: no Ahom Buranji, S.K. Bhuyan (1947) or J.N. Sarkar (1992) text was read
directly; they are known only through Wikipedia's citations. The PIB PDF (2022) and indianculture.gov.in story could not be read (binary/empty fetch).

## Subject in brief
In 1667 Aurangzeb appointed Raja Ram Singh of Amber to retake Guwahati and Kamrup from the Ahom kingdom. The Ahoms under their commander (Borphukan)
Lachit lost badly at Alaboi in August 1669 (about 10,000 dead). In 1671 the decisive fight came on the water at Saraighat, where the Brahmaputra
narrows to about 1 km between hills (Kamakhya, Itakhuli). The Mughals had large boats, some with cannons; the Ahoms used small boats. Lachit and the
Ahom admiral were seriously ill; Ahom boats wavered; Lachit is said to have risen from his sickbed and gone out himself, and the boats rallied. Small
boats and a bridge of boats allowed a front-and-rear attack; the Mughal admiral Munnawar Khan was killed and the fleet broke. The Mughals withdrew
(pursued to the Manas). Lachit died about a year later (April 1672, Kaliabor). Since 1999 the NDA awards a Lachit Borphukan Gold Medal to its best cadet.

## Central question
How did small Ahom boats beat a bigger, cannon-armed Mughal fleet? **Answer:** by fighting where the river is narrowest, so size became a handicap.

## Disputed / legend (qualifications)
- Lachit going out in the boat himself (C07): sources tell it as "said to"; Wikipedia's account first has him watching from his sickbed at Itakhuli. Spoken with "कहते हैं".
- How Munnawar Khan died (C10): gunshot (LHI, Wikipedia Saraighat) vs arrow (Wikipedia Munnawar Khan). Weapon not stated.
- Battle date: mid-March 1671 (Wikipedia) vs "early 1671" (LHI); a Jorhat page says 1672 (snippet). Only "1671" spoken.
- Troop/boat numbers (C17): infobox gives 35,000+ infantry, 18,000 cavalry, 40 ships; the text and footnotes contradict each other. Dropped.
- NDA medal criterion (C15): "best passing cadet" (EastMojo) vs an Assam Tribune snippet suggesting best cadet in officer-like qualities, distinct from the overall topper. Spoken as "बेस्ट कैडेट".

Uncle beheading as fact (C14); poppy seeds (C18); Mir Jumla as commander (C19, debunked); 1672 date (C20); troop and fleet numbers (C17); the Phukanship quote (C22);

## Most surprising verified facts (hand-off)
1. The decisive stretch of the Brahmaputra is only about 1 km wide (C08).
2. The commander was seriously ill during the battle (C06) — and died a year later (C12).
3. The Mughal admiral was killed and the fleet broke (C10).
4. The best-cadet medal at the NDA has carried his name since 1999 (C15).

## Visual details

## Sensitivity notes
Lachit is a revered figure in Assam (Lachit Divas): dignified depiction, no gags. The war is a kingdom-vs-empire conquest war, not a Hindu-Muslim story
(the Mughal commander was a Rajput, Ram Singh). No gore; the admiral's death off-frame. The uncle legend is touched only to say evidence is lacking.

## Subject summary
In early 1857 (from late January in Mathura district; February in Awadh) village watchmen (chowkidars) across north India received flat cakes of coarse flour (chapatis) with an instruction to bake more and pass them to the watchmen of the next villages. The chain spread quickly over "all Upper India". British officials were alarmed; the government ordered inquiries but never established who started it, where, or why. Interpretations at the time ranged from a signal of revolt to a charm against cholera, which had hit several districts the previous autumn. The uprising began at Meerut on 10 May 1857, a few months later. The best modern study (Kim A. Wagner, *The Great Fear of 1857*, 2010, as reported by Dash) traces it to Indore as an attempt to ward off cholera and finds no evidence it was a signal for the revolt.

**Central question:** who was sending the chapatis, and why? (Honest answer: nobody established it; the best reading is a cholera charm; a link to the revolt is unproven.)

## Disputed interpretations
- **Signal for revolt** (many officials then; "fiery cross" writers after 1857) vs **cholera/disease charm** (Cave-Browne, Keatinge, Wagner) vs **religion-defilement rumour**, **Lucknow court intrigue**, **hail charm / dyers**. Only the first two are spoken; the rest go to the description.
- Number of cakes per step varied (four in Mathura; two→ten in Awadh).
- Start date: Thornhill "end of January"; Dash "early February"; Keatinge from 12 January (per Dash). Narration gives no date for the Mathura scene.

## Debunked / NOT USED
- C18 "the chapatis carried secret messages / were the proven signal that launched 1857" — no messages found; link unproven.
- C19 Thornhill's recollection that cakes preceded the Vellore mutiny — a "few who remembered"; Dash notes the Vellore rumour was about defiled salt. Not used.
- C20 "Thornhill found chapatis travelling 300 km a night" (Better India snippet) — misattribution; the estimate is Harvey's 100-200 miles (Dash/Wagner).

## Sensitivity notes
1857 is a foundational freedom-struggle event: do not imply the uprising was "caused by bread" or belittle it; present British fear as fear. Villagers and the Delhi court are shown with dignity; the cholera charm is a real practice, described without mockery. No gore at Meerut.

## Facts (high confidence)
- July 1518, Strasbourg (Holy Roman Empire): a woman began dancing in the street and kept on for days (C01, C02).
- Within days more than thirty people were dancing (C03).
- Physicians blamed "overheated blood" and prescribed more dancing; the council opened guild halls, put up platforms in the horse market and the grain market and paid musicians (C05, C06).
- It backfired; the council banned public music and dancing and sent the worst-affected to the shrine of Saint Vitus at Saverne, with red shoes and crosses (C07, C11).
- It faded by early September (C13).
- Background: years of bad harvests, famine, disease (plague, syphilis), very high grain prices (C10).

## Disputed (said with qualification or kept out of the narration)
- **How many danced:** city clerk Sebastian Brant wrote of "about fifty"; the later Imlin chronicle says four hundred (C04). Narration: "dozens", "the crowd only grew".
- **Deaths:** "up to fifteen a day" comes from later chronicles; Strasbourg's own 1518 records mention no deaths (C08, C09). Narration voices both, in that order.
- **The name "Frau Troffea":** from later accounts; a museum press kit suggests it may be invented. Not used in the narration.
- **Cause:** mass psychogenic illness (Waller) is the leading theory, not settled; Bartholomew reads it as ritual (C12). Narration: "The leading theory".

## NOT USED
- C14: "hundreds danced to death" / any toll as fact.
- C15: ergot poisoning as the cause (Waller rejects it: it restricts blood flow and does not make people dance for days).

## Confidence
High for date, place, the first dancer, the stage + musicians, the ban, Saverne, the famine background; medium for "for days" and the records detail; low for any death figure (voiced only as a claim).

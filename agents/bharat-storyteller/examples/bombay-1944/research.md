## Subject summary
On 14 April 1944 the British freighter SS Fort Stikine, berthed at No. 1 Victoria Dock, Bombay, caught fire in its No. 2 hold in the early afternoon. It carried ammunition/explosives, cotton bales, oil and gold bullion (about £890,000 in 31 crates). The Bombay Fire Brigade fought the fire with hoses through the afternoon; just after 4 p.m. the ship exploded, and a second explosion followed about half an hour later. Fire spread from the docks into neighbouring civilian areas over more than a mile. Hundreds died (estimates from an official ~740 to 800+ and up to ~1,300); the Bombay Fire Brigade lost 66 officers and men (MCGM). Gold bars were flung into the city; most of the gold was later recovered, and small bars turned up again in harbour dredging in 2011. 14 April is observed as Fire Service Day in the firemen's memory; a memorial column stands at the Fire Brigade HQ, Byculla.

**Central question:** what did Bombay lose that day that was worth more than the gold that fell on it — and why does India stop on 14 April?

## Disputed interpretations
- Death toll: official ~740 (Deccan Herald snippet) / 800+ (Wikipedia, Scroll, Amusing Planet) / up to 1,300 (HISTORY.com, Wikipedia lead). Narration: "सैकड़ों".
- Firemen: 66 (MCGM — the brigade's own record; LiveHistory; Better India; Wikipedia body) vs 71 (Wikipedia lead, some fire-service pages). Narration: 66, attributed to the Bombay Fire Brigade.
- Explosives tonnage: ~1,400 t vs ~300 t. Narration: "टनों".
- Gold quantity/bars: £890,000 / 31 crates agreed; bar counts (124, 155) and weights vary. Narration: "सोने के बक्से".
- 2011 find: 31 Jan (OneIndia) vs February (Wikipedia); dredger hopper vs digging a new dock. Narration: year only.
- Firefighting duration: "over a month" (MCGM) vs "three days and nights" (Better India, Amusing Planet). Not spoken.

- **Structure**: forgotten-hero (previous: roopkund mystery-reveal, iron-pillar unexpected-science, saraighat one-decision)
- **Central question**: What did Bombay lose on 14 April 1944 that was worth more than the gold that rained on it?

## C Research report
Summary of `research.md` / `research.json` (23 claims; web-verified 2026-11-02).
- **Facts**: 14 April 1944, SS Fort Stikine at No. 1 Victoria Dock, Bombay, carrying explosives, cotton, oil and ~£890,000 gold in 31 crates (C01-C04); fire noticed ~14:00 (C06), fought with hoses through the afternoon (C07); first explosion just after 4 p.m. (C08), second ~30 min later (C09); fire spread into civilian areas over more than a mile (C10); hundreds killed (C11); 66 Bombay Fire Brigade officers and men died (C14); 14 April is Fire Service Day in their memory (C16); memorial column at Fire Brigade HQ, Byculla (C17); most gold recovered (C12); small bars found again in 2011 dredging (C13).

- **NOT USED / debunked**: C23 "gold rained everywhere and people got rich" (no source); C22 Girgaum roof anecdote (single source); clock stopped at 4:06 (single source).

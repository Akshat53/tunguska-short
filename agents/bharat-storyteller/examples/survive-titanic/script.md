Description:
It's 2:20 AM on 15 April 1912. The Titanic has just gone under, and YOU are in the North Atlantic. The water is about −2 °C (28 °F).

What happens to your body, second by second:
• The first minute: cold shock. You gasp and hyperventilate without meaning to; a gasp under a wave can make you inhale water.
• About 10 minutes (often less in water this cold): your arm and leg muscles stop working, so you can no longer swim or hold on well (the "1-10-1" rule: about 1 minute, 10 minutes, 1 hour; it is a memory aid, not a stopwatch).
• Titanic carried 20 lifeboats with room for about 1,178 people, and many were launched partly empty (Lifeboat 7 left with about 28 people in a boat for 65). About 1,500 people died; about 705-712 survived.
• Lifeboat 14, under Fifth Officer Harold Lowe, is described as the only boat that went back to search for survivors. It pulled a few people from the water.
• The twist: chief baker Charles Joughin said he spent over two hours in the water and lived. Legend says whisky saved him; in his own testimony he spoke of a drink of liqueur, and scientists doubt alcohol helped (it makes you lose heat faster). Historians think he kept his head and upper body out of the water, with his life jacket.
